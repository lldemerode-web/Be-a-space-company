How to Play

Your mission: You are in charge of NASA. Every turn represents one year. Make smart decisions, keep the public happy, manage NASA's budget and fuel, hire specialists, and complete space missions. The game is single-player, and your progress saves automatically on this device.

1. Understand your resources

  • Cash (MD): Your available money, measured in millions of dollars. You spend it on cards, fuel, and launches. You start with 10 MD.
  • # Funding: Determines how much money NASA receives each year. Every 0.5% on the funding track gives 1 MD per year. You begin with 5 MD in annual funding (equivalent to 2.5% on the track). Raising funding helps pay for future missions.
  • Happiness: How happy the public is with NASA, from 0% to 100%. You start at 75%. Happiness changes in steps of 5%, and it cannot exceed 100%.
  • Fuel (MG): Fuel is measured in millions of gallons. You start with 0 MG. Launches require fuel, so you will need to buy or earn it.
  • Fuel Price (FP): The cost of one MG of fuel. It starts at 1.5 MD per MG. Certain cards change the price, but buying fuel does not change it.

2. How a year works

  1. Receive funding: At the start of the year, collect money based on your current # funding level.
  2. Card phase: You may draw one card, then play up to one card from your hand. Both are optional. Some cards cost money or fuel; others change happiness, funding, fuel price, or your team.
  3. Action phase: Choose one action for the year: launch a mission, gain 3 MD, draw two cards, or buy fuel.
  4. Advance the year: Existing launch countdowns progress, completed missions give rewards, and the next year begins.
  5. Check the result: If you meet the victory conditions, you win. If happiness falls too low, NASA closes.

3. Your four actions

  • 🚀 Start a launch: Choose one of ten missions. You must have enough cash and fuel to pay its full cost when you begin.
  • 💵 Gain 3 MD: Add three million dollars to your cash immediately.
  • 🃏 Draw two cards: Add up to two cards from the deck to your hand. Save them for future years.
  • ⛽ Buy fuel: Buy any affordable amount at the current FP. For example, at 1.5 MD per MG, buying 2 MG costs 3 MD. In this digital version, purchases use 0.25 MG increments.

4. Launching space missions

Orbital Research is required: Before launching any rocket, play the Orbital Research card for 10 MD. It unlocks your next two launch starts. After starting two launches, Orbital Research automatically switches back to No and you must play another Orbital Research card to launch again. A Yes/No status indicator on the game screen shows whether launches are currently unlocked.

There are 10 launch types, from Sounding Rocket to Crewed Mars Mission. Each mission shows four things: its money cost, fuel cost, number of years, and rewards. More ambitious missions generally take longer and cost more, but give larger rewards.

Pay when you start: Starting a launch uses your action for that year and immediately deducts its cash and fuel costs. You can have multiple missions underway at the same time.

Wait for completion: The year you start a mission does not count toward its countdown. A three-year mission started in Year 2 still has three years remaining that year; it progresses during Years 3, 4, and 5, completing in Year 5.

Collect rewards: When a mission completes, you receive its listed cash, happiness, and # funding rewards, and your successful-launch total increases by 1. Rewards are not paid when you first start the mission.

5. Playing decision cards

You start with one card. During the card phase, you can optionally draw one new card and play up to one card. Read each card carefully before playing: some increase happiness or funding, some change fuel prices, some provide fuel, and Recruitment cards let you hire an employee. Cards may require cash, fuel, or a particular employee to work well.

If you don't want to play a card, that's fine—continue to your action. You can hold cards for later years.

6. Hiring employees

Recruitment cards let you hire specialists for NASA. Your employee board tracks the people you've hired. Different jobs help in different situations:

  • Astronaut: Can reduce launch preparation costs.
  • Media Relations: Makes Sensitive Talk free.
  • Presidential Relations: Improves the reward from Presidential Call.
  • Engineer: Supports inspection-related abilities.
  • Expert: Makes Explain About Space free and improves Science Breakthrough.

7. Winning and losing

🏆 You win only when BOTH are true: You have completed at least 6 successful launches, and happiness is between 90% and 100%.

⚠️ You lose: If happiness reaches 10% or less at any point, NASA shuts down. Having six launches does not save you if happiness is too low.

8. Strategy tips

  • Don't spend all your money on launches without saving enough to buy fuel.
  • Funding increases are especially valuable early because they earn you more money every future year.
  • Starting missions early lets their countdowns finish sooner, but long missions tie up resources.
  • Use cards and employees to raise happiness, reduce costs, and make your launches easier to afford.
  • Keep an eye on happiness even if your launches are going well: you need at least 90% to win.

🃏 Selling cards

Sell any card from your hand for 1 MD at any time during an active game. Selling does not use your card play or yearly action.

🚀 Rocket failure

Every MG required by a rocket adds 2 percentage points to its failure chance. Having an Engineer reduces the chance by 5 percentage points (minimum 0%). The game checks failure when the mission finishes. Failed missions give no rewards and do not count as successful launches.
